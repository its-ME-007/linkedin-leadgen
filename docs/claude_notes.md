# LinkedIn Search Grammar — Commercial/Retail Property Requirement Posts

Built from analysis of ~17 confirmed "seeking space" posts pulled via Crustdata keyword search (Aug 2026 batch). Use this as the reference for building search queries — manually on LinkedIn, or programmatically via your extension/connector.

---

## 1. Anatomy of a genuine requirement post

Nearly every real post follows the same skeleton. This structure is more reliable than any single keyword.

```
[TITLE LINE — usually ALL CAPS or bold, with a location]
    e.g. "COMMERCIAL PROPERTY REQUIRED – NOIDA"
    e.g. "🏢 Commercial Property Requirement | Well-Established QSR Brand"
    e.g. "🚨 Retail Space Requirement – Hyderabad | [Brand]"

[BRAND/CONTEXT LINE]
    "[Brand] is expanding across [Region]"
    "[Brand] is actively looking for..."
    "As part of our continued Retail Expansion, we are actively seeking..."

[REQUIREMENT BLOCK — bulleted, spec-heavy]
    📍 Location: ...
    📐 Area Required: X–Y Sq. Ft.
    🏪 Frontage: Minimum X ft.
    🏢 Floor: Ground Floor only / Preferred
    ⚡ Power load: X KVA
    🚗 Parking: ...

[CALL TO ACTION]
    "Property owners / brokers / developers with suitable options
     are requested to connect / DM / share details"
    Phone number and/or email often included
```

The **title line + requirement block** combination is your strongest signal — stronger than any single phrase.

## 3. Spec/qualifier terms (the noise filter)

This is the single biggest lever for signal-to-noise. Genuine requirement posts almost always contain at least one of these — marketing/hiring/news posts usually don't.

- "sq ft" / "sq. ft" / "Sq. Ft." / "square feet"
- "carpet area"
- "frontage"
- "ground floor"
- "high footfall"
- "high visibility"
- "main road frontage"
- "power load" / "KVA"
- "rent budget" / "rent asking"

**Recommended baseline query:**
```
("property requirement" OR "commercial property requirement" OR "office space requirement" 
OR "retail space requirement" OR "space requirement") 
AND ("sq ft" OR "sq. ft" OR "carpet area" OR "frontage")
```

## 6. Exclusion terms (strip out the non-leads)

About 3 of every 20 keyword-matched posts are noise. Exclude:

**Supply-side posts (someone offering space, not seeking it):**
- "for lease" (as a title/offer, not "lease options considered")
- "available for" 
- "space for lease"
- "up for grabs"
- Posts starting with an address/area + price, ending in "connect for details" — these are brokers marketing inventory, the mirror image of what you want

**Market commentary / news (not a lead at all):**
- "leased" (past tense, reporting)
- "H1 2026" / "H2 2026" / market stat language
- "read more here" / "lnkd.in" link-only posts

**Recommended exclusion add-on:**
```
-"for lease" -"available for" -"space for lease" -"read more"
```

## 9. Notes for extension design

- **Filter after retrieval, not just in the query** — even the balanced query pulls ~15% noise (broker inventory listings, market news). Consider a secondary classifier step: does the post contain an "ask" (seeking) vs an "offer" (for lease)? Presence of "we are looking for" / "seeking" vs. "for lease" / "available" is the cleanest binary signal.
- **Dedup on author + near-identical text** — the Monterow post appeared twice (posted by two different accounts, likely a broker and a company page), so dedupe on content similarity, not just URL.
- **Structured fields worth extracting per post:** Brand, Property Type (Retail/Office/QSR/Hotel/Warehouse), City, Area (sq ft range), Frontage, Floor preference, Urgency, Contact method.
